AODA website compliance guide

What the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act requires of public sector websites, in plain language.

What is the AODA?

The Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act, 2005 (AODA) is Ontario's law for removing barriers for people with disabilities. Its stated goal was an accessible Ontario by 2025. Detailed requirements are set out in the Integrated Accessibility Standards Regulation (IASR), Ontario Regulation 191/11.

Who has to comply?

  • The Government of Ontario and the Legislative Assembly.
  • Designated public sector organizations. This includes every municipality, school board, college, university, hospital, public transportation organization and the provincial agencies named in the regulation.
  • Large organizations. Private and non-profit organizations with 50 or more employees in Ontario also have website obligations.

What the IASR requires for websites

Section 14 of the IASR requires designated public sector organizations to make their internet websites and web content conform with WCAG 2.0.

DeadlineRequirement for designated public sector organizations
January 1, 2014New internet websites and web content on those sites must conform with WCAG 2.0 Level A.
January 1, 2021All internet websites and web content must conform with WCAG 2.0 Level AA, except live captions (1.2.4) and pre-recorded audio description (1.2.5).

The requirement covers web content published after January 1, 2012. That includes documents such as PDFs posted on your website.

Related obligations

  • Accessible formats and communication supports (section 12). Provide information in an accessible format when someone asks.
  • Accessible feedback (section 11). Your feedback processes, including web forms, must be accessible.
  • Training (section 7). Staff need training on accessibility standards and the Ontario Human Rights Code.
  • Multi-year accessibility plan (section 4). Publish a plan on your website, review it at least every five years, and post an annual status report.
  • Compliance reporting. Designated public sector organizations file accessibility compliance reports with the province on a regular schedule.

What happens if you don't comply?

The province can inspect organizations and issue compliance orders. The Act allows fines of up to $100,000 per day for corporations, and up to $50,000 per day for individuals and directors. For a municipality, the reputational cost of shutting residents out of services can be just as serious.

Where to start

  1. Run a free scan to get a baseline.
  2. Commission a full audit covering templates, key journeys and documents.
  3. Fix the highest-impact issues first, starting with templates that affect every page.
  4. Train content editors so new content stays accessible.
  5. Monitor monthly and record progress for your compliance report.

Note: This guide is general information, not legal advice. Always confirm your obligations against the current text of the AODA and O. Reg. 191/11 on ontario.ca/laws.
